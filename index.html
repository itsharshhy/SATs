Five rules that raise scores

  1. Answer everything. There is no penalty for a wrong answer.
  2. Be careful in Module 1. It sets the difficulty, and the ceiling, of Module 2.
  3. Flag and move on. You can return to any question in the same module.
  4. Use Desmos. The built-in graphing calculator solves many systems and quadratics in seconds.
  5. Open the reference sheet. Area, volume and triangle formulas are built in.

Algebra

1. Slope and lines

y = mx + b

  • m is the slope: rise ÷ run, or (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁).
  • b is the y-intercept, where the line crosses the y-axis.
  • In word problems, the rate ("per hour", "each month") is m and the one-time fee is b.
  • Parallel lines have the same slope. Perpendicular slopes multiply to −1.

Algebra

2. Systems of equations

  • Different slopes: exactly one solution.
  • Same slope, different intercept: parallel, no solution. Match the x and y coefficients but not the constant.
  • Same line: every coefficient and the constant are in the same ratio. Infinitely many solutions.
  • To solve, add or subtract the equations to cancel one variable (elimination), or substitute.
  • Quick check: type both equations into Desmos and click where the lines cross.

Advanced Math

3. Parabolas

  • Vertex form y = a(x − h)² + k shows the vertex (h, k). Watch the sign flip on h.
  • Factored form y = a(x − 1)(x − 5) shows the roots, 1 and 5.
  • The vertex sits halfway between the roots.
  • Sum of roots = −b/a. Product of roots = c/a.
  • a > 0 opens up (vertex is a minimum). a < 0 opens down (vertex is a maximum).

Advanced Math

4. The discriminant

For ax² + bx + c = 0, compute b² − 4ac.

  • "Exactly one solution" means set the discriminant equal to 0 and solve for the constant.
  • A line meeting a parabola once? Substitute one equation into the other, then use the same test.

5. Linear vs exponential

t01234
Linear (+3 each step)3691215
Exponential (×2 each step)124816
  • Linear adds the same amount each step. Exponential multiplies by the same factor.
  • Exponential form: y = a · bᵗ. a is the starting value; b is the multiplier.
  • Grows 6% → b = 1.06. Shrinks 6% → b = 0.94.
  • "Doubles every 3 years" → 2^(t/3). The exponent counts 3-year periods.

Problem Solving & Data

6. Percents and averages

$80 × 1.25 = $100 → $100 × 0.80 = $80

  • +25% then −20% is not +5%. Percent changes multiply, so here there is no change at all.
  • Increase by p%: multiply by (1 + p/100). Decrease: multiply by (1 − p/100).
  • Percent change = (new − old) ÷ old. Always divide by the original.
  • "x is what percent of y?" → x ÷ y × 100.
  • Mean = total ÷ count. Missing value? Find the total first.
  • Outliers pull the mean; the median barely moves.

Geometry

7. Circles

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

  • (x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 25 → center (2, −1), radius 5.
  • Given x² + 4x + y² …? Complete the square: add (4 ÷ 2)² = 4 to both sides.
  • Circumference = 2πr. Area = πr².
  • Arc length = (angle ÷ 360) × 2πr. Sector area = (angle ÷ 360) × πr².
  • π radians = 180°.

Trigonometry

8. Right triangles and trig

  • SOH CAH TOA: sin A = opp/hyp = 3/5. cos A = adj/hyp = 4/5. tan A = opp/adj = 3/4.
  • a² + b² = c² for any right triangle.
  • Complementary angles: sin x° = cos(90 − x)°.
  • 45-45-90 sides: x, x, x√2. 30-60-90 sides: x, x√3, 2x.
  • Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides.

Standard English Conventions

9. Joining sentences

It rained. We stayed in.Correct
It rained; we stayed in.Correct
It rained, so we stayed in.Correct
We had one plan: stay in.Correct
It rained, we stayed in.Comma splice
The recipe calls for: flour and eggs.No full sentence before the colon
  • A semicolon works exactly like a period: complete sentences on both sides.
  • A comma alone cannot join two sentences. Add a FANBOYS word: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.
  • A colon needs a complete sentence before it. After it can come a list, an example or an explanation.

Standard English Conventions

10. Extra information

The violinist, who trained in Vienna, played first.Correct
The violinist — who trained in Vienna — played first.Correct
The violinist (who trained in Vienna) played first.Correct
The violinist — who trained in Vienna, played first.Mixed pair
  • Extra information sits between a matching pair: two commas, two dashes or two parentheses.
  • Test: delete the middle part. The sentence must still work.
  • If the extra information opens with a dash, the answer closes it with a dash.

Craft & Structure · Information & Ideas

12. Predict before you look

  1. Read the short passage and the question. Find the clue: a colon, a "but", an example, a definition.
  2. Fill the blank with your own plain word, like "confirmed" or "simple".
  3. Now look at the choices and pick the closest match to your word.
  • For evidence questions, the right answer can always be proved from the text. Sounding smart does not count.
  • For notes questions (rhetorical synthesis), read the goal first. Choose the sentence that does exactly that goal.
  • For main idea questions, the answer covers the whole text, not one detail.

Test day

Bag checklist

    On the day

    • Eat breakfast and arrive early.
    • Answer every question. There is no penalty for guessing.
    • In Module 1, slow down on easy questions. Careless misses cost the most here.
    • If a question takes more than two minutes, guess, flag it and come back.
    • Use the 10-minute break to drink water and reset. Do not think about Reading and Writing during Math.
